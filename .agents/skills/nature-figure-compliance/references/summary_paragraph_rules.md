# Nature Summary Paragraph Rules

Use this when drafting or reviewing a Nature-style summary paragraph.

## Target Structure

The paragraph should move from broad context to specific result and then back to significance:

1. One or two sentences introducing the field for scientists in any discipline.
2. Two or three sentences of more detailed background for scientists in related disciplines.
3. One sentence stating the general problem addressed.
4. One sentence summarizing the main result, often using "here we show" or equivalent.
5. Two or three sentences explaining how the result changes, extends, or directly compares with previous knowledge.
6. One or two sentences placing the result in a general context.
7. Optional broader-perspective sentences if they materially improve accessibility.

## Length

- Typical target: about 190 to 250 words.
- Upper bound from the local guide example: up to 300 words when broader perspective is included and improves accessibility.

## QC Questions

- Can a scientist outside the field understand the opening?
- Is the problem stated before the result?
- Is the main result concrete rather than promotional?
- Does the paragraph say how the result changes prior understanding?
- Are broader implications present but not overclaimed?
