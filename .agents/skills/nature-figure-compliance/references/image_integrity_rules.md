# Image Integrity Rules

Use these rules for microscopy, photographic panels, gels, blots, and any image-derived scientific data.

## Core Principle

The final image must correctly represent the original data. Presentation may clarify data but must not remove, add, obscure, selectively enhance, or misrepresent evidence.

## Image Assembly

- Do not combine images gathered at different times or from different samples into a single continuous image.
- If juxtaposition is scientifically necessary, mark borders clearly and describe the assembly in the legend.
- Clearly indicate insets and added elements.
- Apply contrast or brightness changes to the whole image and equally to controls.
- Check for duplicated panels. If intentional duplication is used, label it explicitly.
- Do not use generative AI, content-aware editing, cloning, healing, erasing, or masking tools on image data.

## Gels and Blots

- Provide original uncropped data when requested.
- Arrange raw gels in the same order as the final figure.
- Preserve intensity and contrast so raw data match the final representation as closely as possible.
- Mark vertical lane splicing with visible separation or a line.
- Avoid horizontal splicing within a single gel panel.
- Do not mask elements or remove/add data.
- Do not adjust contrast in a way that removes bands, background details, or relative intensity information.

## Local Discussion-Derived Emphasis

The local Nature Cell Biology editorials emphasize that "data beautification" is a real risk: making a story look cleaner by selecting, simplifying, or digitally enhancing data can become misrepresentation even without intent to deceive. For this skill, report concerns plainly instead of polishing them away.
